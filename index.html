Problem 1(a) — Dimensional Homogeneity

Given: $y = a\,e^{-bt}$, where $y$ has units of kg/L and $t$ is time. Find the units of $a$ and $b$.

For an exponential term, the exponent must be dimensionless:

$$[bt]=1$$

Therefore,

$$[b]=[t]^{-1}=T^{-1}$$

So $b$ can have units such as $$\boxed{s^{-1}\text{ or hr}^{-1}}$$

Since $e^{-bt}$ is dimensionless,

$$[a]=[y]$$

Given $y$ has units kg/L,

$$\boxed{[a]=\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}}}$$

Answer: $[b] = \text{s}^{-1}$ or $\text{hr}^{-1}$;   $[a] = \text{kg/L}$

Problem 1(b1) — Velocity Conversion

Convert: $1100\ \text{ft/s}$ to miles/hr.

$$1100\,\frac{ft}{s}\left(\frac{1\ mile}{5280\ ft}\right)\left(\frac{3600\ s}{1\ hr}\right)$$

$$=\boxed{750\ mile/hr}$$

Answer: $750\ \text{miles/hr}$

Problem 1(b2) — Volumetric Flow Rate Conversion

Convert: $400\ \text{in}^3/\text{day}$ to $\text{cm}^3/\text{min}$.

Use $$1\,in=2.54\,cm$$ Therefore $$1\,in^3=(2.54)^3=16.387\,cm^3$$

Hence $$400\frac{in^3}{day}\left(\frac{16.387\,cm^3}{in^3}\right)\left(\frac{1\,day}{1440\,min}\right)$$

$$=\boxed{4.552\ cm^3/min}$$

Answer: $4.552\ \text{cm}^3/\text{min}$

Problem 1(b3) — Length Conversionwatch direction

Convert: $1.8\ \text{nm}$ to inches.

$$1.8\,nm=1.8\times10^{-9}m$$

Since $$1\,in=0.0254\,m$$ $$1.8\times10^{-9}m\left(\frac{1\,in}{0.0254\,m}\right)$$

$$=\boxed{7.087\times10^{-8}\ in}$$

Important: multiplying by $0.0254$ would be the wrong direction for this conversion.

Answer: $7.087\times10^{-8}\ \text{in}$

Problem 2 — Significant Figures & Scientific Notation

Express in scientific notation and give the number of significant figures: 12.44, 5300, 0.034

1. $12.44$ $$12.44=1.244\times10^1$$ Significant figures: $$\boxed{4}$$

2. $5300$ — without a decimal point, trailing zeros are ambiguous. $$5300=5.3\times10^3$$ So normally: $$\boxed{2\text{ significant figures}}$$ If written as $5.300\times10^3$, it has $$\boxed{4\text{ significant figures}}$$

3. $0.034$ — move decimal two places: $$0.034=3.4\times10^{-2}$$ Therefore: $$\boxed{2\text{ significant figures}}$$

Answer: 4 SF; 2 SF (or 4 SF if written with trailing zeros shown as significant); 2 SF

Problem 3 — Moles of NaOH

Given: $m=4\,lb$, $MW=40\ lb/lbmol$. Find (i) lb-moles and (ii) g-moles.

(i) Pound-moles $$n=\frac{m}{MW}$$ $$n=\frac{4}{40}$$ $$\boxed{n=0.10\ lbmol}$$

(ii) Gram-moles — convert lb to g: $$4\,lb\left(\frac{453.592\,g}{1\,lb}\right)=1814.37\,g$$ Now $$n=\frac{1814.37}{40}$$ $$\boxed{n=45.36\ gmol}$$

Answer: $0.10\ \text{lb-mol}$;   $45.36\ \text{g-mol}$

Problem 4 — Average Molecular Weight of Gas Mixtures

Case 1 (dry air): $21\%\ \text{O}_2$, $79\%\ \text{N}_2$.   Case 2: $10\%\ \text{H}_2$, $11\%\ \text{O}_2$, $79\%\ \text{N}_2$.

Use $$\boxed{\bar M=\sum y_iM_i}$$

Case 1: Air $$y_{O_2}=0.21,\qquad y_{N_2}=0.79$$ $$\bar M=(0.21)(32)+(0.79)(28)$$ $$=6.72+22.12$$ $$\boxed{\bar M=28.84\ g/mol}$$

Case 2 $$y_{H_2}=0.10,\quad y_{O_2}=0.11,\quad y_{N_2}=0.79$$ $$\bar M=(0.10)(2)+(0.11)(32)+(0.79)(28)$$ $$=0.20+3.52+22.12$$ $$\boxed{\bar M=25.84\ g/mol}$$

Answer: Air $= 28.84\ \text{g/mol}$; ternary mixture $= 25.84\ \text{g/mol}$

Problem 5 — Mass and Mole Fractions of a Water–NaOH Mixture

Given: $5\,kg\ H_2O$ and $5\,kg\ NaOH$.

Total mass: $$m_T=5+5=10\,kg$$

Mass fractions $$w_{H_2O}=\frac{5}{10}=0.50 \qquad w_{NaOH}=\frac{5}{10}=0.50$$ Therefore $$\boxed{w_{H_2O}=0.50,\qquad w_{NaOH}=0.50}$$

Mole fractions — for water: $$n_{H_2O}=\frac{5000}{18}=277.78\,mol$$ for NaOH: $$n_{NaOH}=\frac{5000}{40}=125.00\,mol$$ Total: $$n_T=277.78+125=402.78\,mol$$

Thus $$x_{H_2O}=\frac{277.78}{402.78}=0.6897 \qquad x_{NaOH}=\frac{125}{402.78}=0.3103$$ Final: $$\boxed{x_{H_2O}\approx0.690} \qquad \boxed{x_{NaOH}\approx0.310}$$

Answer: $w_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} = w_{\text{NaOH}} = 0.50$;   $x_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \approx 0.690$, $x_{\text{NaOH}} \approx 0.310$

Problem 6 — Aqueous Sulfuric Acid Solution

Given: $C=0.5\,mol/L$, $SG=1.03$, $Q=1.25\,m^3/min$, $MW_{H_2SO_4}=98\,g/mol$.

(a) Mass concentration $$C_m=(0.5)(98)$$ $$=49\,g/L$$ Since $1\,g/L=1\,kg/m^3$, $$\boxed{C_m=49\,kg/m^3}$$

(b) Mass flow rate of solution — density: $$\rho=SG(1000)=1.03(1000)=1030\,kg/m^3$$ Convert flow rate: $$1.25\,m^3/min\left(\frac{1}{60}\right)=0.020833\,m^3/s$$ Therefore $$\dot m=\rho Q=(1030)(0.020833)$$ $$\boxed{\dot m_{solution}=21.46\,kg/s}$$

(c) Mass flow rate of $H_2SO_4$ $$\dot m_{H_2SO_4}=(49)(1.25)\left(\frac{1}{60}\right)$$ $$\boxed{\dot m_{H_2SO_4}=1.021\,kg/s}$$

(d) Mass fraction $$w_{H_2SO_4}=\frac{\dot m_{H_2SO_4}}{\dot m_{solution}}=\frac{1.021}{21.46}$$ $$\boxed{w_{H_2SO_4}=0.0476} \quad\text{or}\quad \boxed{4.76\ wt\%}$$

Answer: $C_{\text{mass}}=49\ \text{kg/m}^3$; $\dot m_{\text{soln}}=21.46\ \text{kg/s}$; $\dot m_{\text{acid}}=1.021\ \text{kg/s}$; $w_{\text{acid}}=0.0476\ (4.76\%)$

9. Material Balance

General Balance Equation — Single Compartment

In words: Accumulation = In − Out + Generation − Consumption, taken over the system boundary between times t₁ and t₂.

  • System boundary — the defined control volume across which mass entering/leaving is tracked.
  • Inlet / outlet streams — flows crossing the boundary, driving the change in the total mass retained inside the control volume.

Steady State vs Unsteady State

  • Steady-state system — Accumulation = 0 (nothing changes with time inside the control volume).
  • Unsteady-state system — Accumulation ≠ 0 (conditions change with time).

Multi-Compartment Material Balance

i.e. Σ(mass in) − Σ(mass out) = Accumulation, applied compartment by compartment / species by species.

Practice Problems

Problem 7 — Unsteady-State Storage Tank Balance

Given: $F_1=10000\,kg/hr$, $P=12000\,kg/hr$, $\Delta M=6000\,kg$, $\Delta t=3\,hr$. Find $F_2$.

Use the basic transient balance: $$\text{Accumulation}=\text{Input}-\text{Output}$$

Therefore: $$6000=(10000+F_2-12000)(3)$$ Divide by 3: $$2000=10000+F_2-12000$$ $$2000=F_2-2000$$

Hence $$\boxed{F_2=4000\,kg/hr}$$

Answer: $F_2 = 4000\ \text{kg/hr}$

Problem 8 — Steady-State Mixing of Two Aqueous Streams

Given: $F_1=9000\,kg/hr$, $F_2=1000\,kg/hr$. At steady state: $$\boxed{\text{Input}=\text{Output}}$$

Step 1: Overall balance $$W=F_1+F_2$$ $$W=9000+1000$$ $$\boxed{W=10000\,kg/hr}$$

Step 2: NaOH balance — from feed 1: $0.05(9000)=450\,kg/hr$; from feed 2: $0.50(1000)=500\,kg/hr$. Total NaOH: $$\dot m_{NaOH}=450+500=950\,kg/hr$$ Therefore $$w_{NaOH}=\frac{950}{10000}$$ $$\boxed{w_{NaOH}=0.095}\ \text{or}\ \boxed{9.5\ wt\%}$$

Step 3: Water balance $$\dot m_{H_2O}=(0.95)(9000)+(0.50)(1000)$$ $$=8550+500$$ $$\boxed{\dot m_{H_2O}=9050\,kg/hr}$$ Therefore $$w_{H_2O}=\frac{9050}{10000}$$ $$\boxed{w_{H_2O}=0.905}\ \text{or}\ \boxed{90.5\ wt\%}$$ Check: $0.095+0.905=1$ ✓

Answer: $W = 10000\ \text{kg/hr}$, $w_{\text{NaOH}} = 0.095$, $w_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} = 0.905$

Problem 10 — Air Separation / Waste Stream Split

Given: Feed air ($21\%\ \text{O}_2$, $79\%\ \text{N}_2$); waste is $20\%$ of feed; $25\%$ of the fed O₂ ends up in the waste. Find the waste composition.

Take a basis: $$F=100\,mol$$ Therefore: $$n_{O_2}=0.21(100)=21\,mol \qquad n_{N_2}=0.79(100)=79\,mol$$ Waste is $20\%$ of feed: $$W=20\,mol$$

25% of oxygen is discarded: $$n_{O_2,W}=0.25(21)=5.25\,mol$$ Therefore nitrogen in waste: $$n_{N_2,W}=20-5.25=14.75\,mol$$

Waste composition $$y_{O_2}=\frac{5.25}{20}=0.2625$$ $$\boxed{y_{O_2}=26.25\%}$$ $$y_{N_2}=\frac{14.75}{20}=0.7375$$ $$\boxed{y_{N_2}=73.75\%}$$ Check: $26.25+73.75=100\%$ ✓

Answer: Waste is $26.25\%\ \text{O}_2$, $73.75\%\ \text{N}_2$

Problem 14 — Atomic / Elemental Material Balance

Given: Feed $F=100\%\,NH_3$; product analyzes $17\%\,N_2$, $50\%\,H_2$, $33\%\,NH_3$. Let total product flow be $P$.

Nitrogen balance — one mole $NH_3$ contains one N atom. Therefore $$F=\left[2(0.17)+1(0.33)\right]P$$ $$F=(0.34+0.33)P$$ $$\boxed{F=0.67P}$$

Hydrogen balance — one mole $NH_3$ contains 3 H atoms. Therefore $$3F=\left[2(0.50)+3(0.33)\right]P$$ $$3F=(1.00+0.99)P$$ $$\boxed{3F=1.99P}$$ The slight mismatch is because the product percentages are rounded.

Answer: $F = 0.67P$ (from N); $3F = 1.99P$ (from H) — small mismatch is due to rounding of the product percentages.

Problem 9 — Degree of Freedom Analysis on a Gas Blending System

Given: Blend $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$, $\text{N}_2$ from feeds $F_1$ ($20\%\ \text{CH}_4$, $80\%\ \text{N}_2$), $F_2$ ($10\%\ \text{C}_2\text{H}_6$, $90\%\ \text{N}_2$), $F_3$ ($100\%\ \text{N}_2$) into product $F_4$ with $\text{CH}_4:\text{C}_2\text{H}_6 = 1.5:1$.

Unknowns: $$F_1,\ F_2,\ F_3,\ F_4$$ and two independent product mole fractions. Therefore $$N_U=4+2=6$$

Independent equations — three component balances: $$N_B=3$$ One composition-ratio relation: $$\frac{x_{CH_4}}{x_{C_2H_6}}=1.5$$ so $$N_R=1$$ Total equations: $$N_E=3+1=4$$

Therefore $$N_D=N_U-N_E$$ $$N_D=6-4$$ $$\boxed{N_D=2}$$

Interpretation — there are 2 degrees of freedom. Take a basis such as $F_4=100\,mol$; this fixes one degree of freedom. One more independent specification is required, e.g. one feed flow rate or total product amount: $$\boxed{\text{One additional specification is required after choosing a basis}}$$

Answer: $N_D = 2$. Choosing a basis (e.g. $F_4=100\ \text{mol}$) uses up one; one further specification (a feed rate or total product amount) is needed.

11. Reaction Engineering — DOF & Extent of Reaction

Problem-Solving Steps

  1. Write the material balance / rate equations
  2. Calculate a basis
  3. Account for stoichiometry / specifications
  4. Account for implicit relations

Degree of Freedom for a Reacting System

Nu = number of unknowns, NE = number of independent equations.

ConditionResult
Nu = NEND = 0 → uniquely determined (single, unique solution)
Nu > NEND > 0 → under-specified (infinite solutions)
Nu < NEND < 0 → over-specified (no consistent solution)

Extent of Reaction (ξ)

ni = moles of species i present after reaction  ·  ni0 = moles of species i initially present  ·  νi = stoichiometric coefficient of species i (negative for reactants, positive for products)

Practice Problems

Problem 11 — Extent of Reaction (CO Oxidation)

Reaction: $$2CO+O_2\rightarrow2CO_2$$ Feed: $20\ \text{mol CO}$, $10\ \text{mol O}_2$; $15\ \text{mol CO}_2$ formed. Find $\xi$.

For $CO_2$, $$\nu_{CO_2}=+2$$ Given: $$n_{CO_2,0}=0 \qquad n_{CO_2}=15\,mol$$

Use $$\xi=\frac{n_i-n_{i0}}{\nu_i}$$ Thus $$\xi=\frac{15-0}{2}$$ $$\boxed{\xi=7.5\,mol}$$

Answer: $\xi = 7.5\ \text{mol}$

Problem 13 — Extent of Reaction for Ammonia Synthesis

Reaction: $$N_2+3H_2\rightarrow2NH_3$$ Inlet: $18\ \text{mol H}_2$, $15\ \text{mol N}_2$. Outlet: $9\ \text{mol H}_2$, $12\ \text{mol N}_2$, $6\ \text{mol NH}_3$. Use $$\xi=\frac{n_i-n_{i0}}{\nu_i}$$

Using $H_2$: $\nu_{H_2}=-3$ $$\xi=\frac{9-18}{-3}$$ $$\boxed{\xi=3\,mol}$$

Using $N_2$: $\nu_{N_2}=-1$ $$\xi=\frac{12-15}{-1}$$ $$\boxed{\xi=3\,mol}$$

Using $NH_3$: initially $n_{NH_3,0}=0$, $\nu_{NH_3}=+2$ $$\xi=\frac{6-0}{2}$$ $$\boxed{\xi=3\,mol}$$ All three agree: $$\boxed{\xi=3\,mol}$$

Answer: $\xi = 3\ \text{mol}$ (consistent across all three species — good check on the balance)

12. Limiting Reagent, Conversion, Selectivity & Yield

  • Excess Reagent (ER) — any reactant present in an amount greater than that required to react with the limiting reagent.

Problem 12 — Limiting Reactant and Maximum Extent

Reaction: $$C_7H_{16}+11O_2\rightarrow7CO_2+8H_2O$$ Feed: $1\ mol\ C_7H_{16}$, $12\ mol\ O_2$.

13. Combustion System Terminology

  • Flue gas / stack gas — all gases resulting from a combustion process.
  • Orsat analysis (dry basis) — flue-gas composition reported excluding water vapour.
  • Theoretical (stoichiometric) air — the amount of air required to completely combust all combustible elements to their highest oxidation state, with no leftover oxygen.
  • Complete combustion — all combustible elements are oxidized to their highest oxidation state (C → CO₂, H → H₂O).
  • Partial / incomplete combustion — produces CO and/or unburned carbon.

Problem 15 — Propane Combustion and Percent Excess Air two methods compared

Reaction: $$C_3H_8+5O_2\rightarrow3CO_2+4H_2O$$ Given: $20\,kg\ C_3H_8$, $400\,kg\ air$.

Step 1: Moles of propane $$n_{C_3H_8}=\frac{20}{44}$$ $$=0.4545\,kmol$$

Step 2: Theoretical oxygen required — from stoichiometry, $1\ mol\ C_3H_8 : 5\ mol\ O_2$. Thus: $$n_{O_2,theo}=5(0.4545)$$ $$=2.2727\,kmol$$ Mass of theoretical oxygen: $$m_{O_2,theo}=2.2727(32)$$ $$\boxed{m_{O_2,theo}=72.73\,kg}$$

Method exactly as written in the notes — using $21\%$ directly on the air mass: $$m_{O_2,in}=0.21(400)=84\,kg$$ Then $$\%\,excess=\frac{84-72.73}{72.73}\times100$$ $$\boxed{\%\,excess=15.5\%}$$

Physically correct air calculation — air is approximately $21\ mol\%\ O_2$, not $21\ wt\%$. $$w_{O_2}=\frac{0.21(32)}{0.21(32)+0.79(28)}$$ $$=0.2330$$ Therefore: $$m_{O_2,in}=0.2330(400)$$ $$=93.20\,kg$$ Hence $$\%\,excess\ air=\frac{93.20-72.73}{72.73}\times100$$ $$\boxed{\%\,excess\ air=28.14\%}$$

So for an exam following the handwritten-note method, the expected answer may be 15.5%, but the physically correct calculation gives 28.14%.

Answer: Quick (weight-percent-shortcut) method gives $15.5\%$; the thermodynamically correct calculation gives $28.14\%$ excess air.

Problem 16 — Stoichiometric Oxygen Requirement for a Fuel Mixture

Given: Take a basis: $100\,mol\ \text{fuel gas}$. Components: $19.5\%\,C_3H_8$, $59.4\%\,C_4H_{10}$, $21.1\%\,C_5H_{12}$.

For $C_nH_m$, theoretical oxygen is $n+\frac{m}{4}$ mol $O_2$/mol fuel.

Propane: $3+\frac{8}{4}=5$. Oxygen required: $19.5(5)=97.5\,mol$

Butane: $4+\frac{10}{4}=6.5$. Oxygen required: $59.4(6.5)=386.1\,mol$

Pentane: $5+\frac{12}{4}=8$. Oxygen required: $21.1(8)=168.8\,mol$

Total $$n_{O_2,theo}=97.5+386.1+168.8$$ $$\boxed{n_{O_2,theo}=652.4\,mol}$$ for $100$ mol fuel gas. Therefore: $$\boxed{6.524\ mol\ O_2/mol\ fuel}$$

Answer: $652.4\ \text{mol O}_2$ per $100\ \text{mol}$ fuel gas, i.e. $6.524\ \text{mol O}_2/\text{mol fuel}$

14. Recycle, Purge & Bypass Systems

  • Recycle — a portion of an output stream is returned and combined with the fresh feed.
  • Purge — a small stream is bled off from a recycle loop to prevent the build-up of inerts or impurities.
  • Bypass — a stream skips a process unit entirely and rejoins the process downstream.

Definitions

fov (overall conversion) is based on fresh feed to the whole process; fsp (single-pass conversion) is based on the LR actually entering the reactor (fresh feed + recycle).

Problem 17 — Multi-Unit Fermentation Process multi-block balance, under-specified

Given: A 3-block lactic-acid fermentation process — Block 1 (Mixer): water $F_1$ + glucose $F_2$ → $F_3$ (80% water, 20% glucose). Block 2 (Reactor): $F_3$ + Lactobacillus $F_4$ → $F_5$ (76.9% water, 19.2% glucose, 3.85% lactic acid). Block 3 (Separator): $F_5$ + vitamins $F_6$ → $F_7$ (96.2% water) + $F_8$ (83.3% glucose).

The supplied material gives the process balances but does not provide enough numerical specifications to obtain unique values for all stream flow rates — the correct solution is the balance formulation rather than inventing missing data.

Block 1: Mixer — streams: $F_1=$ pure water, $F_2=$ pure glucose, $F_3=80\%$ water + $20\%$ glucose.
Water balance: $$F_1=0.80F_3$$ Therefore $$\boxed{F_1=0.80F_3}$$ Glucose balance: $$F_2=0.20F_3$$ Therefore $$\boxed{F_2=0.20F_3}$$ Overall balance: $$F_1+F_2=F_3$$ Substituting: $0.80F_3+0.20F_3=F_3$, which checks.

Block 2: Fermenter — feed streams $F_3,\ F_4$; outlet $F_5$. Total balance: $$F_3+F_4=F_5$$ Therefore $$\boxed{F_5=F_3+F_4}$$

Block 3: Separator/Purifier — inputs $F_5,\ F_6$; outputs $F_7,\ F_8$. Overall balance: $$F_5+F_6=F_7+F_8$$ $$\boxed{F_5+F_6=F_7+F_8}$$

Water balance (using the compositions supplied): $$0.769F_5=0.962F_7$$ Thus $$\boxed{F_7=\frac{0.769}{0.962}F_5}$$ $$\boxed{F_7\approx0.7994F_5}$$

Glucose balance: $$0.192F_5=0.833F_8$$ Hence $$F_8=\frac{0.192}{0.833}F_5$$ $$\boxed{F_8\approx0.2305F_5}$$ These are the directly supported relations from the supplied problem statement.

Answer: The balance equations above relate every stream to $F_5$ (or $F_3$); the problem as stated does not fix a numerical basis, so a unique numerical solution requires one more specification (e.g. a basis flow rate) — the balance formulation is the complete correct answer without inventing missing data.
